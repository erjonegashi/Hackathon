Typical Appliances & Power Ratings
How Many Watts Does A Washing Machine Use? | EnergySage
Kosovo households use a variety of devices. Common appliances range from low-power LEDs (~5–15 W) to heavy loads (kilowatt-scale). For example, washing machines draw roughly 400–1,400 W
, dishwashers about 1,800 W
, and refrigerators 300–800 W
 when running. TVs typically use 50–200 W
, while an electric kettle is around 1,200–1,500 W
. Smaller devices (lights, phones, laptops) use far less, but major appliances dominate consumption. Kosovo utility data note that laundry (mainly washers) accounts for ~8.7% of a home’s electricity bill, dishwashers ~7.8%, and the refrigerator ~4.8%
. These figures guide our device inventory.

Washing Machine: ~400–1,400 W (e.g. 900 W washer ⇒ ~140 kWh/year at 3x/week)
.
Dishwasher: ~1,800 W per cycle
.
Refrigerator: ~300–800 W compressor draw
 (runs cyclically, ~1–1.5 kWh/day).
Television (LED): ~50–200 W
.
Electric Kettle: ~1,200–1,500 W
 (boiling ~1L).
Lighting: ~5–15 W per LED bulb (incandescent ~60 W).
Others: e.g. laptop (50–100 W), vacuum (~500–1,200 W), air conditioner (if present) (500–2,500 W).
All devices’ power ratings (watts) are used to compute instantaneous load. For example, turning on a 1.5 kW kettle and a 50 W lamp yields 1,550 W. Multiplying by time gives kWh used. Using this inventory, we can model household power draw from “on/off” states of appliances.

Kosovo Electricity Tariffs & Time-of-Use Rates
Kosovo’s regulator (ERO) mandates time-of-use tariffs for homes
. There are two slots: high (day) and low (night) tariff hours. High-tariff (day) periods are 07:00–22:00 (Oct–Mar) and 08:00–23:00 (Apr–Sep)
; night hours outside these windows are low-tariff. Tariffs are tiered by consumption (via an 800 kWh/mo threshold) and by time-of-day
. Under the latest rates (2025/26):

Up to 800 kWh/mo: ~€0.0905/kWh at day rates.
Above 800 kWh: ~€0.1543/kWh at day rates.
Night (off-peak) rates (23:00–08:00): ~€0.0388/kWh (≤800 kWh) and €0.0728/kWh (>800 kWh)
.
A small fixed monthly fee per connection also applies
. This tariff structure means that shifting usage into off-peak or keeping total consumption under 800 kWh has big savings. (Exact rates are set annually by ERO
.)
Grid Data & Demand Signals (KEDS/KOSTT)
Kosovo’s grid is managed by KEDS (distribution) and KOSTT (transmission/market). Both issue public alerts but have no open consumer API. Key signals include:

KOSTT Daily Load: The System Operator publishes real-time load data on its website and via the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform
. This provides system-wide demand curves for every day.
Peak Warnings: In winter 2025, KOSTT warned of record consumption and identified peak hours (9–12 h, 16–20 h, and 22–23 h)
. They urged shifting appliance use to off-peak (night) times. KEDS similarly appeals to customers during grid crunch (e.g. a Nov. 2025 appeal cited record demand >1,400 MWh and asked for savings)
.
No Public API: There is no known public REST API for Kosovo grid data. A consumer app would rely on published forecasts or ENTSO-E/KOSTT data feeds and operator announcements to trigger demand-response events.
Estimating Household Consumption from Device States
We can build a simple model: track each device’s on/off status and power rating to compute household load. For instance, if a 600 W heater and a 100 W lamp are on, instant load is 700 W. Integrating over time yields energy (kWh). Established datasets help validate such models. For example, the UCI “Household Power Consumption” dataset provides sub-metered loads by category (e.g. kitchen appliances, laundry/fridge, water-heating/AC)
. This shows how devices combine into total usage. Using it, we can calibrate realistic usage patterns (e.g. a wash cycle ~0.5–1 kWh). The app could let users input which appliances are running (or detect via smart plugs) and sum their known wattages to estimate real-time load and monthly total. It then computes savings as the difference between this estimate and a baseline (e.g. projected usage if the appliance had stayed on).

Gamified DR Flow & Rewards Design
We propose a game-like demand-response workflow based on proven pilot programs:

OhmHour Challenges: When a peak event is predicted (e.g. 17:00–18:00), push a notification asking users to unplug or defer loads for one hour (“OhmHour”).
Goals & Feedback: Show each user a personalized baseline vs. reduction target. Provide a live progress bar or dashboard tracking kWh saved. For example, Bidgely’s HomeBeat app displayed the user’s baseline and goal, real-time savings progress, and compared it to neighbors
. Immediate feedback (e.g. green highlight, confetti) is given when targets are hit.
Tiered Rewards: Convert saved energy (“negawatts”) into points or digital currency. Allow redemption for gift cards, tokens, or bill credits. Use tiered incentives to reward consistency: e.g. small reward for meeting the goal each event, with bonus for multi-event streaks. (In a 2016 Australian trial, participants saved ~30% during DR events, earning up to ~$25 per event, with tiered payouts encouraging sustained effort
.)
Community Leagues: Organize users into teams by building or neighborhood. Show leaderboards of total CO₂/kWh saved or points earned. This social competition boosts motivation.
UI Rewards: Use engaging visuals (e.g. react-native-confetti-cannon) to celebrate achievements. For example, when a user hits a savings goal, trigger a confetti animation. This positive reinforcement (like game “level-up”) enhances engagement.
Together, these elements create an engaging demand-response experience. By combining real-time device tracking (with Kosovo’s tariffs and grid alerts) and gamified incentives
, the app empowers Kosovars to save electricity at peak times and earn rewards.
